GymTracker privacy policy

Last updated 5 October 2026

GymTracker keeps your records on your own device. There is no account, and your records never leave it. Only two things ever come to us: a report when something in the app breaks, and anything you choose to post on the app's Feedback page.

What the app keeps

Everything you enter stays in a database on your device: your workouts, sets, runs and other activities, your routines, the names of people you train with, and your settings, such as units and language.

  • Exports are files you choose to create and choose where to send.
  • Automatic backups are written once a day to a place you control: a folder you pick on Android, iCloud Drive or the Files app on iPhone, or your Documents folder on Windows. We cannot see them.
  • Reports you print or save as PDF go wherever you send them.

The app does not use your location, camera, microphone or contacts.

Error reports

When something goes wrong in the app, such as a screen that fails or the app closing unexpectedly, it sends a report to our own server so the problem can be found and fixed. A report contains:

  • the error itself: its type, its message and where in the app's code it happened
  • which screen you were on, and a few internal identifiers, such as the id of the item on screen (never what it contains)
  • the app's version, your device's model and its operating-system version
  • a device identifier (explained below)

A report never contains your workouts, the names of people you train with, your notes, or anything you typed. Reports are kept on our server for 30 days, apart from a few kept longer as examples of each problem, so that a problem can still be investigated if it comes back.

Your choice. Error reports are on unless you turn them off in Settings → Error reports. Turning them off also deletes any reports still waiting on your device.

Feedback

The app's Feedback page lets you report a problem or suggest a feature, and vote for other people's suggestions. When you post, we receive what you wrote, whether it is a problem or an idea, the app's version and your platform, together with the device identifier. If you post from the error screen, or from the note shown after the app closed unexpectedly, the post is linked to that error report.

Posts are checked by us before anyone else sees them. Once approved, a post is shown to other people using GymTracker, without your name or anything else about you. We may correct a post's wording or hide it, for example to remove personal details. The app does not ask for your name or email, so please don't include them. Posts and votes are kept until we remove them; to have a post of yours removed, write to the address below.

The device identifier

Error reports and feedback carry an identifier for your device, so that we can tell how many devices a problem affects, show you your own posts, and allow one vote per device. It is made on your device by scrambling, in a way that cannot be reversed, the identifier your phone gives this app together with the app's name. We never receive your phone's own identifier, the identifier is different in each of our apps, and it is not linked to your advertising ID or used for ads.

Our server is hosted by Amazon Web Services in the United States. Connections to it are encrypted. The server limits how often each internet address can post and vote, without storing the address with your reports or posts; like most web servers, it keeps a standard access log, which includes IP addresses, for security and troubleshooting.

Store ratings

Now and then, after you finish a workout, the app may ask Google Play or the App Store to show their own rating prompt. The store decides whether to show it and handles your rating; the app never learns what you chose. On Android, Settings also has a link to the app's store page.

Ads, on Android and iPhone

The Android and iPhone versions show ads from Google AdMob: a banner at the bottom of the screen, and one full-screen ad at most once a day after you finish a workout. The ads come from Google, not from us, and Google collects some information from your device to show them, count them and prevent fraud:

  • your device's advertising identifier (on Android, the advertising ID; on iPhone, the IDFA, only if you allow tracking when the app asks)
  • your IP address, which Google uses to estimate roughly where you are
  • information about your device and the app, such as the model, the operating system and the app's version
  • how you interact with the ads, and crash and performance information

None of your workouts or other records are sent to Google or anyone else. How Google uses this information is explained at How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Your choices. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks the first time you open the app whether ads may be personalised, and you can change your answer at any time in Settings → Privacy choices. On iPhone, Apple asks whether the app may track you, and you can change that in the iPhone's Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. If you say no, you still see ads, but they are not based on your activity in other apps and websites. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in the phone's settings.

Remove ads

Remove ads is a one-time purchase made through Google Play or the App Store. They handle the payment, and we never see your card or payment details. The app only asks the store whether your account owns Remove ads, and saves the answer on the phone. Once ads are removed, the app stops requesting ads from Google altogether.

The Windows version

The Windows version shows no ads and sells nothing. Apart from the error reports and feedback described above, nothing leaves your computer unless you export it.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
